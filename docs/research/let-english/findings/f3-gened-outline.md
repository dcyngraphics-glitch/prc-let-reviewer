# Facet f3-gened-outline — LET General Education coverage and CHED CMO mapping

**Research date:** 2026-11-22 · **Geography:** Philippines · **Scope:** current rules; older frameworks flagged.
**Deliverable:** findings only (no final report). Citations `[n]` map to URLs in the **Sources** section; only pages actually opened are cited. Search-result snippets were used as leads only, never as citations.

---

## 1. Bottom line / key correction

1. The **current, authoritative Gen Ed blueprint** is the *Table of Specifications (TOS)* issued by the Professional Regulatory Board for Professional Teachers: **BPT Resolution No. 11, s. 2025**, amending Resolution No. 11, s. 2022. It defines **10 Gen Ed areas × 15 items each = 150 items**, one table shared by Elementary and Secondary, with a 30% easy / 50% moderate / 20% difficult mix mapped to Bloom's taxonomy and the seven PPST domains.[1][8]
2. **There is a direct conflict with the older PRC "coverage of examination" lists**, which still describe Gen Ed as **5 areas** (English, Filipino, Mathematics, Science, Social Sciences). This 5-area wording appears in a PRC program PDF for January 2022[2] and in boardexams.ph's March 2026 reminders[3] — i.e. it is still being republished as "current" in 2026. No source opened explicitly reconciles the two lists. Treat this as an unresolved disagreement, not a settled fact.
3. **CMO 82 s. 2017 is NOT the General Education curriculum CMO.** CHED's own 2017 CMO index identifies CMO No. 82, s. 2017 as *"Policies, Standards and Guidelines for Bachelor of Culture and Arts Education (BCAEd)"*.[4] The PRC TOS cites **CMO 75 s. 2017 (BSEd)** and **CMO 74 s. 2017 (BEEd)**, not CMO 82.[1][8] The LET Gen Ed content itself descends from **CMO 20, s. 2013** ("General Education Curriculum: Holistic Understandings, Intellectual and Civic Competencies"), which set 8 core courses + 1 mandated Rizal course + 9 elective units.[5][6][7]
4. Practical consequence for the app: a CMO-82-based categorization would be mis-targeted. Use the **10 TOS areas** as categories, with the **legacy 5-area names as aliases** for ingested reviewer-book chapters.

### 2.2 Legacy: 5-area "coverage of examination" (still republished)
PRC program PDF, January 2022:[2]
1. **English** — Study and Thinking Skills; Writing in the Discipline; Speech and Oral Communication; Philippine Literature; Master Works of the World.
2. **FILIPINO** — Komunikasyon sa Akademikong Filipino; Pagbasa at Pagsulat tungo sa Pananaliksik; Masining na Pagpapahayag.
3. **MATHEMATICS** — Fundamentals of Math; Plane Geometry; Elementary Algebra; Statistics and Probability.
4. **SCIENCE** — Biological Science – General Biology; Physical Science – with Earth Science.
5. **SOCIAL SCIENCES** — Philippine Government/New Constitution with Human Rights; Philippine History; Basic Economics, Taxation, Agrarian Reform; Society, Culture with Family Planning; Rizal and Other Heroes; Philosophy of Man; Arts; General Psychology; Information and Communication Technology.

The identical list is carried by boardexams.ph for the **March 2026** LET.[3] Several commercial reviewers also use the 5-area framing.[11][13][16][17]

**Weights (both frameworks agree):** Gen Ed is **40% of Elementary** and **20% of Secondary**; passing requires a general average of ≥75% with no test below 50%.[2][3][8][10] Elementary has Gen Ed + Prof Ed; Secondary adds Specialization.[1][2]

## 3. Explicit CHED CMO mapping

| LET Gen Ed area (TOS 2025) | CMO 20 s. 2013 GE core/mandated course | Legacy 5-area home |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Purposive Communication in English | Purposive Communication / Malayuning Komunikasyon (English medium) | English (Study & Thinking Skills; Writing in the Discipline; Speech & Oral Communication) |
| 2 Malayuning Komunikasyon sa Wikang Filipino | Purposive Communication / Malayuning Komunikasyon (Filipino medium) | Filipino (Komunikasyon sa Akademikong Filipino; Pagbasa at Pagsulat; Masining na Pagpapahayag) |
| 3 Readings in Philippine History and Society | Readings in Philippine History | Social Sciences (Philippine History; Philippine Government/New Constitution) |
| 4 The Life and Works of Rizal | Life and Works of Rizal — mandated 3 units | Social Sciences (Rizal and Other Heroes) |
| 5 The Contemporary World | The Contemporary World | No clean legacy equivalent (global/contemporary concerns) |
| 6 Art Appreciation | Art Appreciation | Social Sciences (Arts / Art Appreciation–Humanities) |
| 7 Science and Technology | Science, Technology and Society | Science (Biological/Physical) + Social Sciences (ICT) |
| 8 Mathematics | Mathematics in the Modern World | Mathematics |
| 9 Ethics | Ethics | Social Sciences (Philosophy of Man) |
| 10 Understanding the Self | Understanding the Self | Social Sciences (General Psychology) partly |

Sources for the middle column: the full 2013 GE CMO text (8 core courses, descriptions, 24+9+3 units)[5]; CHED presentation naming it CMO 20 s. 2013 and listing the 8 core courses + Rizal[6][7]. Sources for the third column: the PRC 2022 coverage list[2].

**On CMO 82 specifically:** no Gen Ed mapping exists in the opened sources. CMO 82 s. 2017 is the BCAEd program PSG, one of the 2017 teacher-education CMOs 74–82.[4] Its only demonstrable LET relevance is indirect: from September 2025 the secondary specialization *Culture and Arts Education* replaced MAPEH, and that specialization corresponds to the BCAEd program area governed by CMO 82.[10] The TOS's own reference list names CMO 75 s. 2017 (BSEd), RA 7836, DepEd Order 42 s. 2017 (PPST), and RA 10968 (PQF) — not CMO 82.[1][8]

**Why old topics persist:** CHED's 2013 reform guidance says mandated/legislated topics — Philippine Constitution, agrarian/land reform, population education and family planning, taxation, climate change and environmental awareness — were to be *integrated into* the new GE rather than remain standalone subjects.[6][7] That helps explain why those topics appear in the legacy 5-area Social Sciences list but have no standalone TOS category.

## 4. Area-by-area topic outline (for question categories)

For each area: **official outcome** (from TOS[1]), then **concrete topics** reported by opened reviewer sources, then **legacy alias**.

### 4.1 Purposive Communication in English — 15 items[1]
- Official outcomes: use English with ease in effective communication; use it confidently for special purposes.[1]
- Topics reported: subject–verb agreement, parallelism, modifiers; pronoun reference and antecedent agreement; tense consistency; vocabulary in context; reading comprehension of short passages (main idea, inference, author's purpose, vocabulary in context); tone and format for purpose/audience.[9][16]
- Also named in legacy coverage: Study and Thinking Skills, Writing in the Discipline (essay structure, thesis statements, transitions, citation/documentation), Speech and Oral Communication (public speaking, delivery, audience analysis).[2][17]
- Literature note: the **legacy** English area explicitly included Philippine Literature and Master Works of the World[2][17], but the 2025 TOS has no standalone literature category; literature may surface through reading comprehension. This is a source disagreement in emphasis — reviewers split on whether Gen Ed English is "communication-focused, not literature"[16] or includes literature[17].

### 4.2 Malayuning Komunikasyon sa Wikang Filipino — 15 items[1]
- Official outcomes (verbatim): "Nagagamit ang wikang Filipino sa larangang akademiko"; "Nagagamit ang wikang Filipino sa iba't - ibang uri ng sulatin".[1]
- Topics reported: tamang baybay (KWF Ortograpiyang Pambansa); tamang gamit ng pang-uri at pang-abay; tamang gamit ng pang-ugnay (at, ngunit, dahil, bagaman, kaya, samakatuwid); "ng" vs "nang"; pormal vs impormal na Filipino; pag-unawa sa binasa.[9]
- Further Filipino topics reported: kayarian ng pangungusap (paksa/panaguri), uri ng pangungusap (payak, tambal, hugnayan, tambal-hugnayan), bantas, gamit ng salita/formal and informal registers;[16] gramatika at estruktura ng wika (parts of speech, affixation, tenses, sentence patterns); barayti at baryasyon ng wika (region, social class, context); panitikan (tula, maikling kuwento, sanaysay, dula, nobela; authors, literary periods, figures of speech); reading comprehension, research writing and documentation.[14]
- Legacy aliases: Komunikasyon sa Akademikong Filipino; Pagbasa at Pagsulat tungo sa Pananaliksik; Masining na Pagpapahayag.[2] One reviewer writes "Masining na **Pamamahayag**" instead of "Pagpapahayag"[14] — term disagreement, flag both. boardexams.ph writes "Akademikong **Pilipino**" while PRC writes "Akademikong **Filipino**".[2][3]

### 4.3 Readings in Philippine History and Society — 15 items[1]
- Official outcomes: understand significant periods of Philippine history; relate those periods to the transformation of society.[1]
- Period/subject checklist reported: pre-colonial (barangay, datu, baybayin); Spanish period (Magellan 1521, Legazpi, galleon trade, encomienda); Revolution (Katipunan 1892, Biak-na-Bato 1897, June 12 1898 independence); American period (Treaty of Paris 1898, Tydings–McDuffie 1934, Commonwealth 1935); Japanese occupation/WWII; post-independence, Martial Law 1972, EDSA 1986, 1987 Constitution.[9] Another reviewer gives a similar sequence including British occupation 1762 and Rizal's 1896 execution.[16]
- Skill: reading and interpreting **primary sources** (proclamation, letter, account) is in the CMO 20 course description and in reviewer guidance.[5][9]
- Legacy aliases: Philippine History; Philippine Government / New Constitution with Human Rights; Basic Economics, Taxation, Agrarian Reform; Society, Culture with Family Planning.[2]

### 4.4 The Life and Works of Rizal — 15 items[1]
- Official outcomes: analyze how Rizal's thoughts, works and valor influenced the nation; show how his feats influenced other Philippine heroes.[1]
- Topics reported: RA 1425 (Rizal Law, 1956); *Noli Me Tangere* (1887) and *El Filibusterismo* (1891) — plots, characters, criticisms; essays, poems, letters; La Liga Filipina; travels, Dapitan exile, trial and execution (Dec 30, 1896).[9] Contributions of other heroes, nationalism and patriotism are covered by the social-science reviewer.[15]
- CMO link: mandated 3-unit course under CMO 20 s. 2013.[5][6] Legacy alias: "Rizal and Other Heroes".[2]

### 4.5 The Contemporary World — 15 items[1]
- Official outcomes: relate global issues/concerns to local and global realities; identify ways the Philippines can participate in mitigating global problems.[1]
- Topics reported: globalization (economic, political, cultural), climate change, migration, inequality, global health, international organizations, Philippine participation.[9] CMO 20 course description: "Globalization and its impact on individuals, communities and nations, challenges and responses."[5]
- No clean legacy alias found; legacy Social Sciences list has no "Contemporary World" entry.[2] Gap noted.

### 4.6 Art Appreciation — 15 items[1]
- Official outcomes: interpret artistic creations as expressions of the finer things in life; informed appreciation of various art forms.[1]
- Topics reported: elements and principles of art; forms — painting, sculpture, architecture, music, dance, theater, film, literature; Filipino artists (Juan Luna, Félix Resurrección Hidalgo, Fernando Amorsolo, Vicente Manansala, José Joya) and the Order of National Artists; traditional arts, kundiman, tinikling, singkil.[9] Broader humanities list: Philippine arts and culture, visual arts, music/dance/theater, literature and creative expression, cultural heritage and preservation.[15]
- CMO 20 description: nature, function and appreciation of the arts in contemporary society.[5] Legacy alias: "Arts" / "Art Appreciation–Humanities".[2]

### 4.7 Science and Technology — 15 items[1]
- Official outcomes: use scientific knowledge to explain natural phenomena and protect Earth's resources; illustrate how technology benefits mankind.[1]
- Topics reported — Biology: cells, photosynthesis, respiration, genetics, ecology, human body systems;[9] cell theory, DNA, dominant/recessive traits.[16] Chemistry: periodic table, bonding, acids and bases.[9] Physics: Newton's laws, energy, waves, electricity.[9][16] Earth science: plate tectonics, weather, water cycle, solar system.[9] STS: benefits and risks of technology, environmental issues.[9]
- Legacy aliases: Biological Science (General Biology); Physical Science with Earth Science; Information and Communication Technology.[2]

### 4.8 Mathematics — 15 items[1]
- Official outcomes: competence in mathematical concepts and procedures; relate math to real/concrete daily-life problems.[1]
- Topics reported: fractions, decimals, percentages (increase/decrease/reverse), ratios and proportions, linear equations and simple systems, word problems (mixture, work, age, distance-rate-time, simple interest), geometry (triangles, circles, area, volume), basic statistics (mean/median/mode, tables and graphs), number patterns and sequences.[9] Additional: number systems, set theory, algebraic expressions/inequalities, factoring, permutations/combinations.[17]
- CMO 20 course: Mathematics in the Modern World — nature of mathematics, practical/intellectual/aesthetic dimensions, application in daily life.[5] Legacy aliases: Fundamentals of Math, Plane Geometry, Elementary Algebra, Statistics and Probability.[2]

### 4.9 Ethics — 15 items[1]
- Official outcomes: understanding of what is good for individuals and society; use logical inferential skills in resolving moral dilemmas.[1]
- Topics reported: virtue ethics (Aristotle), duty-based ethics (Kant), utilitarianism (Bentham, Mill), natural law (Aquinas); facts vs values; steps in resolving a dilemma; Filipino values in moral decisions.[9] Philosophy bucket in the social-science reviewer: major philosophical ideas, ethics and morality, **logic and reasoning**, influential philosophers, educational philosophy.[15]
- **Philosophy/Logic/Humanities placement:** the 2025 TOS has no standalone Philosophy or Logic area. Logic appears as "logical inferential skills" inside Ethics[1] and as an indexed topic in the social-science reviewer[15]; the branches of philosophy (metaphysics, epistemology, axiology, logic) are otherwise treated under Professional Education / philosophical foundations in the opened reviewer sources, not as a Gen Ed category. Flag: do not create a top-level Gen Ed "Philosophy/Logic" category on the strength of the legacy list alone.
- CMO 20 course: Ethics.[5] Legacy alias: "Philosophy of Man".[2]

### 4.10 Understanding the Self — 15 items[1]
- Official outcomes: familiarity with factors/forces affecting self-identity; commitment to self-understanding as means to a successful teaching career.[1]
- Topics reported: philosophical views of self (Socrates, Plato, Augustine, Descartes, Locke, Hume, Kant, Freud); self in sociology/anthropology/psychology (William James's "I" and "me", Carl Rogers's real and ideal self); physical, material, spiritual, political and digital self; learning to learn, goal setting, managing stress.[9]
- CMO 20 description: nature of identity, factors and forces affecting personal identity.[5] No clean legacy alias; closest is "General Psychology".[2] Gap noted.

- **No source opened explicitly reconciles the 5-area program list with the 10-area TOS.** This is the top open question for the app builder.
- No standalone source was opened that gives a per-topic weighting *within* each of the 10 Gen Ed areas beyond 15 items each; the only finer lists opened are reviewer-suggested topic checklists, not official TOS sub-items.
- No official source was opened confirming the exact weights (40%/20%) inside the 2025 TOS itself; those weights come from PRC program announcements and reviewers.[2][3][8][10]
- Philosophy/logic appears in opened sources as reviewer-level topic guidance[15] and as TOS Ethics outcomes[1]; no opened official Gen Ed document defines a "Philosophy/Logic/Humanities" category.
