# Facet F5 — LET Professional Education & Filipino subject scope, and CHED CMO 82 alignment

Research date: 2026-11-22. Geography: Philippines. Scope: current LET/LEPT, with older sources flagged.
Question: What topics are covered in the Professional Education and Filipino subjects of the Philippine LET, and how do they align with CHED CMO 82?
Decision supported: whether/how to include Prof Ed and Filipino practice sets in an English-major LET reviewer.

Citations are numbered from the retrieval ledger at the end. Only pages actually opened are cited; search snippets were treated as leads only.

---

## 1. Exam architecture (needed to interpret the decision)

- RA 7836 §14 defines the structure: elementary examination = two parts (Professional Education + General Education); secondary = three parts (General Education, Professional Education, and a field of specialization) [1][5].
- Weights: elementary examinees take General Education 40% and Professional Education 60%; secondary examinees take General Education 20%, Professional Education 40%, Specialization 40% [1].
- The current blueprint is the Enhanced Table of Specifications (TOS) issued under Board for Professional Teachers (BPT) Resolution No. 11, s. 2022 and amended by BPT Resolution No. 11, s. 2025, published/effective 25 March 2025 [3][5]. The 2025 amendment adds an explicit mapping of every topic/learning outcome to the seven Philippine Professional Standards for Teachers (PPST) domains and integrates Philippine Qualifications Framework Level 6 descriptors; difficulty mix is 30% easy / 50% moderate / 20% difficult [3][5].
- PRC's TOS assigns 150 items each to General Education, Professional Education, and each secondary specialization except Mathematics (120) [3].
- Board Resolution No. 15 (s. 2022) set transition rules for old- vs new-curriculum BEEd/BSEd graduates; the 2023 LEPT onward is open to all BEEd/BSEd graduates regardless of graduation year [2].

Relevance to the decision: Professional Education is compulsory for every elementary and secondary examinee, including English majors [1]. Filipino appears in two distinct roles — as a compulsory General Education component for all examinees, and as a 150-item secondary specialization that only Filipino majors take [1][2][3]. These are different products for a reviewer.

### 2.2 Area-by-area outline (PRC learning outcomes + reviewer elaboration)

**A. Teaching Profession (23 items)** — PRC learning outcomes: apply philosophical and sociological principles in teaching-learning situations; apply foundation theories of special and inclusive education; describe the professional teacher and the means to ensure high standards of personal and professional life; explain teaching and the various teacher roles in meeting 21st-century challenges; understand the teacher as school-culture catalyst, transformational leader and educational resources manager, with responsibilities specified in the Code of Ethics for Professional Teachers [5]. Mapped PPST domains 1, 6, 7 [5]. A reviewer elaboration lists the usual study topics: philosophies of education (idealism, realism, pragmatism, existentialism, perennialism, essentialism, progressivism, reconstructionism), history of Philippine education, key laws (RA 7836, 9293, 9155, 10157, 10533, 10912, 10968, 11476) and the Code of Ethics [4].

**B. Curriculum, Methods, Educational Technology (45 items — the largest block)** — PRC learning outcomes: research-based knowledge of concepts, theories and principles in curriculum planning, design, development and evaluation; teaching strategies that build and enhance new literacies (multicultural, social, media, financial, cyber/digital, ecological, arts-and-creativity); developmentally sequenced lesson plans with well-aligned outcomes and competencies based on the K-to-12 spiral curriculum; new-literacy concepts (globalization and multicultural literacy, social, media, financial, cyber, digital, eco, arts-and-creativity literacy, interdisciplinary explorations) and shared cultural practices; teaching strategies, materials, technology and classroom-management techniques appropriate to subject areas and inclusive of indigenous learners; developing and using conventional and non-conventional resources including ICT for varied learners [5]. Mapped PPST domains 1 and 4 [5]. Reviewer elaboration adds curriculum models (Tyler, Taba, Wheeler, Saylor & Alexander), curriculum types, CIPP and Stake evaluation models, K-to-12/MATATAG, direct/inquiry/problem/project-based/cooperative/differentiated/5E strategies, and TPACK, SAMR, Dale's Cone and Mayer's multimedia principles [4].

**C. Child and Adolescent Learners and Learning Principles (30 items)** — PRC learning outcomes: student-centered, metacognitive, innovative, inclusive and developmentally appropriate pedagogical approaches for child and adolescent learners; appraise learning environments responsive to family background, economic level groupings and socio-cultural affiliation; differentiated teaching for learner gender, strengths, interests, experiences and needs; implications of child-development research along biological, cognitive, linguistic and socio-cultural dimensions [5]. Mapped PPST domains 1, 2, 3, 4 [5]. Reviewer elaboration lists Piaget, Erikson, Kohlberg, Bronfenbrenner, behaviorist/cognitivist/constructivist/social-learning/humanist theories, Gardner's multiple intelligences, classroom management and motivation [4].

**D. Assessment of Learning (22 items)** — PRC learning outcomes: principles of traditional and alternative/authentic high-quality assessment; development and use of formative and summative assessment tools; rules in test construction and authentic product/process assessment tools; interpreting assessment results to improve learning; basic concepts of statistics in educational assessment and evaluation; timely, accurate and constructive feedback to learners and parents [5]. Mapped PPST domain 5 [5]. Reviewer elaboration adds Bloom's revised taxonomy, affective (Krathwohl) and psychomotor (Simpson) domains, validity/reliability, item analysis, norm- vs criterion-referenced grading and rubrics [4].

**E. Field Study and Teaching Internship (30 items)** — PRC learning outcomes: describe authentic experiential learning from field study and actual classroom immersion; teaching assistantship and guided mentored classroom teaching; prepare a portfolio on learning behavior, motivation, classroom management and assessment from direct observation of teaching-learning episodes in an actual school environment; reflective thinking and teaching; identify teaching-learning problems and offer research-based recommendations (action research) [5]. Mapped PPST domains 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 [5].

Earlier program documents (PRC, Sept 2021; and the March 2025 republication) list the same coverage in coarser form: Teaching Profession + Social Dimensions of Education; Principles of Teaching + Educational Technology + Curriculum Development; Facilitating Learning + Child and Adolescent Development; Assessment of Student Learning + Developmental Reading; Field Study + Practice Teaching [1][2]. These older labels map onto the five TOS areas above but are not identical terminology.

## 3. Filipino — sourced topic outline

### 3.1 Where Filipino sits

- In General Education (all examinees), Filipino coverage is: Komunikasyon sa Akademikong Filipino; Pagbasa at Pagsulat tungo sa Pananaliksik; Masining na Pagpapahayag [1][2]. The current TOS renames the first two into "Malayuning Komunikasyon sa Wikang Filipino" (15 of 150 GenEd items) [3][5].
- In the secondary Filipino specialization/major (150 items), the broad PRC coverage is grouped as: **Mga Batayang Teoretikal** — Introduksyon sa Pag-aaral ng Wika, Panimulang Linggwistika; **Nilalaman** — Wika, Panitikan; and **Metodolohiya, Pagtataya at Ebalwasyon, at Kagamitang Pangturo** [1][2].
- Only Filipino majors take the Filipino specialization; an English major's secondary specialization is English [1][2][3]. This distinction drives the recommendation in §5.

### 3.3 Older primary TOS, lettered sections A–S (2022 Annex A — superseded weights, use for topic detail only)

The 2022 PRC Annex A organizes the Filipino specialization into lettered sections A–S with learning outcomes [6]. Sections directly read: A. Introduksyon sa Pag-aaral ng Wika (8%, 11 items); B. Panimulang Linggwistika (5%, 8); C. Ang Filipino ang Kurikulum ng Batayan Edukasyon; K. Barayti at Baryasyon ng Wika (5%, 8); L. Mga Natatanging Diskurso sa Wika at Panitikan (5%, 8); M. Panulaang Filipino (7%, 9); S. Dulang Filipino (3%, 5) [6]. Letters D–J were not extracted [6].

Representative learning outcomes from that source: Section A covers theories of language for effective teaching, the importance of Filipino in the age of technology and K-to-12, language-learning theories/principles, characteristics and levels of language, first language vs wikain, research knowledge, modernization of Filipino, and social interaction using language skills [6]. Section B covers phonology, morphology, syntax and semantics in language teaching, parts of speech in Filipino, and ICT in the development of Filipino orthography [6]. Section Q covers the development and origins of the maikling kuwento and nobela, their elements and types, writers and works, and analysis of Palanca prize-winning Filipino novels using literary theory [6]. Section S covers the historical development of Filipino drama, Balatagsan and Batutian, ICT in teaching drama forms (Dulang Pansilid Aralan, Dulang Adaptasyon, Sabayang Bigkas, Dramatic Monologue, Readers Theater), elements of drama and theatre, and creative strategies [6].

Weights in this 2022 table differ from the 2025 table (e.g., Maikling Kuwento at Nobelang Filipino 7%/9 items in 2022 vs 10%/15 in 2025) — treat 2025 counts as current [3][6].

## 4. Alignment with CHED CMO 82

### 4.1 What CMO 82 actually is

CMO No. 082-17, dated 2 November 2017, is the "Policies, Standards and Guidelines for Bachelor of Culture and Arts Education (BCAEd)" — a teacher-education program PSG, not a generic LET syllabus [7]. Its program outcomes include the outcomes common to the Teacher Education discipline: rootedness of education in philosophical, socio-cultural, historical, psychological and political contexts; mastery of subject matter; facilitating learning through a wide range of methodologies and delivery modes; developing innovative curricula, instructional plans, teaching approaches and resources for diverse learners; applying ICT; thinking skills in planning, monitoring, assessing and reporting learning; professional and ethical teaching standards; and lifelong learning through experiential and field-based opportunities [7]. It also requires graduates to communicate effectively in spoken and written forms in both English and Filipino [7].

CMO 82 §9.1 prescribes a 42-unit Core Professional Education block in three clusters [7]:
- **Foundation / Theories and Concepts (4 subjects):** The Child and Adolescent Learner and Learning Principles (3); The Teaching Profession (3); The Teacher and the Community, School Culture and Organizational Leadership (3); Foundation of Special and Inclusive Education (3).
- **Pedagogical Content Knowledge (6 subjects):** Facilitating Learner-Centered Teaching (3); Assessment in Learning 1 (3); Assessment in Learning 2 (3); Technology for Teaching and Learning 1 (3); The Teacher and the School Curriculum (3); Building and Enhancing New Literacies Across the Curriculum (3).
- **Experiential Learning:** Field Study 1 (3); Field Study 2 (3); Teaching Internship (6).

### 4.2 Mapping Prof Ed TOS → CMO 82 core Prof Ed courses

| LET Prof Ed TOS area | CMO 82 course(s) that cover it |
|---|---|
| A. Teaching Profession (incl. philosophical/sociological foundations, special and inclusive education, Code of Ethics) | The Teaching Profession; Foundation of Special and Inclusive Education; The Teacher and the Community, School Culture and Organizational Leadership [5][7] |
| B. Curriculum, methods and strategies, new literacies, educational technology | The Teacher and the School Curriculum; Facilitating Learner-Centered Teaching; Building and Enhancing New Literacies Across the Curriculum; Technology for Teaching and Learning 1 [5][7] |
| C. Child and Adolescent Learners and Learning Principles | The Child and Adolescent Learner and Learning Principles [5][7] |
| D. Assessment of Learning | Assessment in Learning 1; Assessment in Learning 2 [5][7] |
| E. Field Study and Teaching Internship (incl. reflection, action research) | Field Study 1; Field Study 2; Teaching Internship [5][7] |

The match is close to one-to-one across all five areas. This is consistent with the 2025 TOS's own PPST/PQF integration: PRC states the TOS was designed to align the LEPT with the competencies and program outcomes of the CHED PSGs, and it adopts DepEd Order No. 42, s. 2017 (PPST) [5]. CMO 82's stated outcomes and its Annex C ICT Competency Standards for Teachers reflect the same framework [7].

### 4.3 Alignment for Filipino — important qualification

- No opened source explicitly maps the **Filipino major** TOS to CMO 82. The PRC 2025 TOS cites CHED Memorandum Orders No. 74 (BEEd) and 75 (BSEd), s. 2017, and DepEd Order No. 42, s. 2017, not CMO 82 [5]. CMO 82's own majors are culture/arts courses (Foundations of Culture and Arts Education, Culture and Arts Education in Plural Societies, Perspectives in Philippine Cultural Heritage, Principles and Practices in Creative Expressions I–IV), not a Filipino major [7].
- The only direct CMO 82 → Filipino link found in an opened source is the program outcome requiring graduates to communicate effectively in spoken and written English **and Filipino** [7].
- Therefore: Prof Ed alignment with CMO 82 is strong and demonstrable via the shared core Prof Ed course cluster [5][7]; Filipino-major alignment with CMO 82 is indirect at best and should not be asserted as a direct TOS-to-CMO-82 mapping. See Gap 5.

## 5. Implications for the English-major reviewer decision

- **Include Prof Ed practice sets:** Prof Ed is compulsory for all examinees and carries 60% (elementary) / 40% (secondary) of the rating [1]. It is not major-specific, so English majors must take it. Within it, Area B (45 items) deserves the largest share [3][5].
- **Include Filipino in General Education scope, not as a full major set:** GenEd Filipino is compulsory for every examinee [1][2], and the current GenEd TOS allots 15 items to Malayuning Komunikasyon sa Wikang Filipino [3][5]. The 150-item Filipino specialization table is only for Filipino majors; an English major takes the English specialization [1][2][3]. A 19-topic Filipino-major practice set would therefore be out of scope for the primary English-major audience unless the product deliberately targets Filipino majors too.
- **If a Filipino set is included anyway**, prioritize the three 15-item topics (Istruktura ng Wikang Filipino; Pagtuturo at Pagtataya ng Makrong Kasanayang Pangwika; Maikling Kuwento at Nobelang Filipino) which make up 45 of 150 items [3].
- **Use 2025 item counts, not 2022**, and label any study-topic elaboration as reviewer-derived rather than official where it comes from commercial reviewer sources [3][4][6].

## 6. Gaps and verification notes

1. The official CHED PDF of CMO 82 at `legacy.ched.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/CMO-No.-82-s.-2017.pdf` returned HTTP 502 on fetch; the full text used here is the legaldex reproduction of CHED Memorandum Order No. 082-17 [7]. The annex "Description of Professional Education Courses" is listed but its course-by-course descriptions were not present in the extracted text; a Scribd copy exposed only "You are on page 1 of 49". Detailed CMO 82 course descriptions remain unverified.
2. The PRC January 2022 LET program PDF failed extraction (HTTP 422, "content could not be extracted"); the Sept 2021 PRC program PDF was used for the older coverage lists [1]. Flag those coverage labels as older than the 2025 TOS.
3. The 2025 PRC TOS PDF opened here did not expose a Filipino-major TOS table in its extracted text. The 19-topic/150-item Filipino counts are from prepph.com's stated transcription of the 2025 TOS [3], not read directly from the PRC PDF. Verify against the PRC original if it becomes available.
4. Only lettered sections A, B, C, K, L, M, N, O, P, Q, R and S of the older 2022 Filipino TOS were directly read; D–J were not extracted [6]. Its weights differ from 2025 in several topics.
5. No source found that explicitly ties the LET Filipino major TOS to CMO 82. CMO 82 is a BCAEd PSG and defines no Filipino major [7]; PRC cites CMO 74/75 [5]. Any CMO 82 alignment claimed for Filipino should be limited to the general English-and-Filipino communication outcome [7].
6. The 2025 Prof Ed TOS table is labeled Elementary; no separate Secondary Prof Ed TOS was confirmed in the opened sources. One reviewer source reports the same numbers were used for both levels in 2022 and advises checking prc.gov.ph for a Secondary-specific version [3][4].
7. Several sources surfaced in search but were not opened (topnotcher.ph Filipino reviewer; passthatboard.com Filipino coverage; reviewersph.com LET coverage/open-exam-prep.com). They are leads only and are not cited.
8. supertutor.ph [4] is a commercial reviewer; it is used only for elaborating study subtopics, never as authority for official scope or item counts.
